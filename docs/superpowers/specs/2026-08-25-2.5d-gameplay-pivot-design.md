**Visual authority:** `outputs/just-some-stars-2.5d-gameplay-target-v1.png`

## 1. Decision

*Just Some Stars* changes from stylized full 3D to cinematic layered 2.5D for
both planetary exploration and ship flight. Unity remains the engine and the
completed runtime foundation remains intact. The pivot replaces the character,
environment, movement, camera, flight and rendering implementation from Task
12 onward; it does not restart Tasks 1–11 or the approved Frontend.

The game is not implemented as full 3D viewed from the side. Gameplay uses 2D
physics and authored side-view routes. Depth comes from layered paintings,
foreground occlusion, parallax, light, fog, particles, scale changes and
bounded visual depth lanes.

## 2. Product experience

- Surface play is readable left-to-right exploration with slopes, jumping,
  climbing, jet assistance, moving platforms, interactions and secrets.
- Flight is a cinematic 2.5D route with bounded horizontal/vertical movement,
  optional authored depth-lane transitions, boost, brake/drift, hazards,
  gravity-route choices and landing/departure transitions.
- The Discovery Lens aims inside the composed scene, changes focus between
  declared depth bands and tests in-world phenomena without becoming a quiz.
- Usually the Captain, two companions and Ori are fully active at a
  destination. Other crew contribute through communications.
- The visual composition remains premium and quiet: the route, objective,
  character silhouettes and contextual action are always legible.

## 3. World and rendering architecture

Unity 6 URP uses the 2D Renderer for gameplay scenes. Each destination owns a
`LayeredSceneDefinition` with these required bands:

1. `Sky`
2. `FarWorld`
3. `Atmosphere`
4. `Midground`
5. `Gameplay`
6. `ActorsAndProps`
7. `Foreground`
8. `Hud`

Every layer declares a stable sorting range, parallax factor, camera bounds,
lighting mask and Addressables group. Gameplay collision is authored
separately with simple `Collider2D` shapes and never inferred from painted
alpha.

The renderer uses sprite normal maps, emission masks, restrained 2D lights,
fog cards, pooled particles and destination color grading. Mirra keeps the
approved warm/cold transition. Transparent overdraw and texture residency,
not triangle count, become the principal graphics budgets.

## 4. Character animation architecture

Characters use frame-atlas animation rather than Unity 2D skeletal
deformation. Runtime movement remains smooth at the game frame rate while
illustrated frames normally advance at a deliberate 12–15 FPS cadence.

The runtime contract consists of:

- `SpriteAnimationClipDefinition`: stable id, frame sprites, per-frame
  duration, loop mode, facing, pivots, contacts and frame events.
- `CharacterSpriteSet`: compatible clips and layers for one body family or
  bespoke character.
- `SpriteAtlasAnimator`: deterministic clip playback and event emission.
- `LayeredCharacterRenderer`: synchronizes a bounded Captain layer stack.
- `SpriteFrameEvent`: foot contact, tool attach/detach, interaction, audio and
  VFX cues.

The Captain uses at most five synchronized visual layers: body/base clothing,
head/hair, silhouette-changing costume, backpack/equipment and foreground
hand/tool. Palette masks change skin, hair, suit and Signal colors without
duplicating atlases. Mira, Juno, Kai and Bea use flattened full-body atlases
where possible. Ori owns a separate mechanical atlas contract.

Side-view gameplay normally needs right and left animation families. A strip
may be mirrored only after visual review proves that equipment, patches,
lighting and identity remain correct. Asymmetric characters receive authored
opposite-facing strips. Discovery Lens and special actions use a small declared
aim-pose set rather than sixteen complete body directions.

## 5. Sprite production pipeline

The pipeline adapts the strongest `hatch-pet` mechanisms without using its pet
format:

1. Bind a canonical approved identity image and written character authority.
2. Define a gameplay clip with exact frame count, direction, cadence and
   anchor contract.
3. Generate or draw the entire coherent strip from the declared references.
4. Extract frames deterministically from the original-resolution strip.
5. Normalize one shared scale, baseline and pivot without per-frame size pops.
6. Validate transparency, clipping, accidental holes, component continuity,
   facing, contacts, identity and cadence.
7. Build a versioned atlas and manifest with source and output hashes.
8. Produce contact sheets and looping motion previews.
9. Repair a failed coherent row rather than patching an unrelated one-off
   final frame into the atlas.
10. Import only approved atlases into Unity and verify the real in-game motion.

Image generation can create grounded animation drafts, but deterministic
assembly and visual acceptance own final geometry. Generated identity drift,
reversed cadence, malformed anatomy, sliding baselines and inconsistent props
are blockers even when a structural validator passes.

## 6. Surface gameplay and camera

`SurfaceMotor2D` uses `Rigidbody2D`/`Collider2D` with a fixed simulation step.
It owns horizontal motion, slopes, jump, jet assistance, external forces,
moving surfaces and recovery volumes. Body families share capability and use
only collider/visual calibration; they do not receive different speed or jump
power.

`CompositionCamera2D` follows the active subject with a dead zone, look-ahead,
vertical bounds, authored composition targets, zoom profiles and reduced
motion. It has no free orbit. Camera policy continues through the Task 8 mode
contracts.

Crew navigation uses authored `TraversalGraph2D` nodes and interaction anchors
instead of 3D NavMesh. Off-camera recovery remains bounded and never visibly
teleports an actor inside the composed camera view.

## 7. Flight

Flight is also 2.5D. `FlightMotor2D` moves the ship within an authored route
envelope. Visual depth lanes may change scale, occlusion and hazard ownership,
but collision and decisions remain deterministic. Gravity assists, relative
motion and route planning are preserved through authored paths, moving frames
of reference and prediction overlays rather than unrestricted 3D space.

The flight camera uses composition rails, speed look-ahead and reduced-motion
profiles. Approach, orbit, landing and departure remain short seamless masks
for additive loading.

## 8. Reused and superseded work

### Reused unchanged

- Tasks 1–9: Unity project, builds, services, settings/input, saves, modes,
  streaming, content ids, events and validation.
- Award-level Frontend and local license/privacy surfaces.
- Task 10 character identity, dimensions, roles, equipment, expressions,
  palettes and material semantics.
- Task 11 Blender tooling as an optional deterministic pipeline for limited
  3D props, reference renders or future experiments.

### Superseded as production direction

- Task 12 3D Captain Stages 1–3 are preserved historical work, not inputs to
  the shipping character pipeline.
- Task 12 3D Stage 4 rigging/walk work is stopped and must not continue.
- Humanoid rigs, skin weights, blendshapes, character FBX/LOD requirements,
  third-person surface camera, 3D character IK and unrestricted 3D flight are
  removed from the active plan.

No unfinished Task 12 3D file is deleted during the pivot. The branch remains
recoverable until the user separately authorizes archival or deletion.

## 9. Performance and content loading

The first benchmark determines enforceable per-destination budgets. It must
measure texture residency, sprite-atlas residency, transparent overdraw, 2D
light cost, particle cost, frame time, managed/native memory and scene-load
peaks on the approved Android profile.

Runtime rules:

- Only the current destination and required shared actors remain resident.
- Atlases are grouped by destination and animation family through
  Addressables.
- Source art may be large; runtime imports use verified max size and ASTC
  compression appropriate to observed screen coverage.
- Characters share atlas/material pages where it preserves quality.
- Dynamic 2D lights, fog and full-screen transparency are bounded by the
  benchmark rather than multiplied freely.
- Performance, Balanced, Cinematic and High Frame Rate profiles scale render
  resolution, lighting, particles, fog and post-processing without removing
  gameplay information.

## 10. Verification strategy

Focused verification runs inside each stage. Full project regression and paid
device testing run once at the declared package boundary, not after every art
iteration.

Required gates include:

- EditMode: manifests, frame metadata, pivots, contacts, import settings,
  layer definitions, Addressables ownership and budget validation.
- PlayMode: motor determinism, camera bounds, parallax, interaction anchors,
  animation events, layer synchronization, scene streaming and resume.
- Visual: coherent strips, contact sheets, motion previews, target-comparison
  captures and no identity/cadence/baseline defects.
- Android benchmark: a 20–30 second Mirra route with Captain, one companion,
  Ori, one interaction, complete depth layers, lighting and particles.
- Package boundary: full affected suites, APK build, artifact inspection and
  one bounded Limrun/Argent session only when a new APK is ready.
