## Binding visual contract

- Gameplay is true layered 2.5D with side-view traversal, not a full-3D scene
  shown through a side camera and not one flattened background image.
- The playable plane, interactive silhouettes and route must remain readable
  while foreground, midground, distant terrain, atmosphere and sky create
  parallax depth.
- Mirra preserves the continuous orange-sunset to frozen-blue transition and
  the restrained cyan/violet Signal accents.
- The five children remain distinct 12–14-year-old cinematic-storybook
  characters. Ori remains a small expressive survey robot.
- Character motion uses coherent frame-atlas animation. It must not depend on
  runtime skeletal deformation, skin weights or a 3D Humanoid rig.
- The Captain may use a bounded synchronized sprite stack for customization;
  bespoke crew members use flattened full-body atlases where possible.
- Surface exploration and flight both use authored 2.5D routes. Flight may use
  visual depth lanes, but it does not restore a free third-person 3D camera.
- Lighting, normal/emission masks, particles, fog and color grading add depth
  without obscuring collision, interactions or touch controls.
- Every final gameplay capture is judged against the approved image at the
  actual Android viewport, not only against source art in an image editor.

## Production interpretation

The target is reconstructed from separately owned layers. A valid scene has a
sky, distant world, atmospheric, midground, gameplay, character/prop,
foreground and HUD layer contract. A single screenshot behind moving sprites
does not satisfy approval.

Character sprites follow a game-specific evolution of the `hatch-pet`
principles: canonical identity grounding, coherent animation strips,
deterministic extraction and registration, versioned metadata, transparent
atlas validation, motion previews and explicit visual QA. The Codex pet
`8x11`, `192x208`, nine-state and sixteen-direction packaging rules do not
apply to the game.
