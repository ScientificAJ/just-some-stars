# Character reference approval ledger

Task 10 produced twelve approval-gated character-reference images. The shared
art direction below is approved, but it does not approve any unfinished sheet.
Every image remains blocked from production until its own row is explicitly
approved by the user. After the 2026-08-25 pivot these sheets ground side-view
frame-atlas character art rather than mandatory Blender meshes.

## Locked visual authority

- **Decision:** approved by the user on 2026-08-23.
- **Style name:** cinematic storybook realism.
- **Gameplay-direction reference:**
  `outputs/just-some-stars-2.5d-gameplay-target-v1.png`
  (SHA-256
  `72644970448effd81177222e0aa23ae8a23f9b733077dab6e27e9ca765f5eaed`).
  The earlier `outputs/just-some-stars-mirra-gameplay-target-v1.png` remains
  historical palette, world, material and cast lineage only.
- **Cast age read:** the representative Captain and four named crew—five human
  children total—read as 12–14 years old. Ori is the sixth cast member.
- **Human proportions:** approximately 6–6.5 heads tall, unmistakably
  adolescent, with soft believable facial anatomy, slightly enlarged
  expressive eyes, natural noses and mouths, and modestly enlarged hands,
  gloves, feet and boots for animation and mobile readability.
- **Rendering:** premium cinematic storybook depth with warm appeal and
  believable skin, hair, cloth, rubber, painted plastic, brushed metal, glass
  and emissive material response. The references may depict dimensional
  rendering, but shipping characters are painterly frame-atlas sprites. The
  result is neither photoreal nor toy-like.
- **Construction language:** practical homemade exploration clothing assembled
  by clever neighborhood children: layered canvas and insulation, repaired
  seams, repurposed gear, hand-painted patches, compact packs and readable tool
  attachments. Avoid sterile NASA replicas, superhero armor and military
  tactical styling.
- **World and lighting:** the warm sunset-amber and cool deep-blue starlight
  duality from the gameplay reference, with restrained cyan/violet Signal
  energy, soft cinematic facial light and strong silhouettes.
- **Ori:** an original compact survey robot built from salvaged electronics,
  painted panels and believable mechanical joints, with one expressive optical
  eye and restrained Signal accents. Ori must not read as a generic mascot or
  recognizable franchise robot.
- **Exclusions:** no anime, chibi or toddler proportions; no adult proportions;
  no glossy plastic skin; no generic mobile-game mascot language; no copied
  franchise silhouettes, clothing or robot shapes; no excessive neon.
- **Continuity rule:** every later sheet must preserve this exact age read,
  proportion system, material quality, homemade construction language,
  warm/cool world duality and Signal treatment. A later sheet cannot silently
  reinterpret the approved style.
- **Production-authority rule:** written labels, role descriptions, numerical
  dimensions, landmark diagrams and control notes are authoritative over any
  incidental inconsistency in a generated illustration. Sprite artists,
  animators and deterministic atlas tooling must correct such illustration errors in the real build rather
  than trace them. In particular, Task 29 must implement each named expression
  semantically (for example, a focused closed-mouth `determined` pose and an
  open-eyed compact `speaking` pose), even if a thumbnail's face does not
  perfectly express its label.

## 2.5D production interpretation

The approved identities, measurements, silhouettes, equipment, expressions
and palettes remain binding. Front/side/back sheets are identity-construction
authority; side-view gameplay strips may simplify invisible detail while
preserving silhouette and role. Former shared-rig landmarks now define
compatible sprite pivots, contacts, clothing boundaries and attachment
anchors. No approval here requires runtime bones, skin weights, character FBX
files or 3D LODs.
